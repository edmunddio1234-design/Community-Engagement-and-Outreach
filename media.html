Media & marketing

Say one thing, everywhere, until it sticks

Rural marketing is not a campaign. It is one sentence, repeated by trusted people, in the four or five places that parish actually looks — the church bulletin, the parish paper, the Facebook group, the radio at 6 a.m., and the person at the Council on Aging everybody calls.

The message, before the channels

A campaign with three messages has none.

Everything the enterprise says in these eight parishes for the next year should reduce to one line, and that line is a time and a place, not a mission statement:

The line

“The M-TOC will be at [place], [day], [time]. Bring your DD-214. There is no cost.”

Mission statements do not move rural people to a parking lot. A date does. Everything below — the press list, the story bank, the calendar — is machinery for repeating that one line until people can say it back to you without looking it up.

The rule that makes it work

Fix the rhythm. Same day, same place, every month. “First Saturday, Clinton square” becomes a fact people repeat. A schedule that moves is a schedule nobody learns — and an unlearned schedule is just advertising you have to buy again every month.

Four kinds of channel

Owned

What you control

The deployment calendar page, the earned card, the church bulletin insert, the email list, the eight programme pages, and the Guardian Light memorial. Costs nothing but discipline. Start here — nothing else works until the calendar is published.

Earned

What others say about you

Parish papers, the WBRZ community segment, radio interviews, the police jury minutes. Free, slow, and worth more than everything else combined because it carries someone else’s credibility.

Shared

What people pass on

Parish Facebook groups, church announcements, VFW and Legion word of mouth, the Council on Aging phone tree. The only channel that reaches a veteran who does not read and does not go online.

Paid

What you buy last

Booth fees, printing, a small run of parish-paper ads around the fair dates. Buy nothing until the first three are working — paid reach on an unclear message is money set on fire.

The official-journal insight

Every Louisiana parish designates an official journal — the newspaper that legally prints its minutes and public notices. The Livingston Parish News holds it for Livingston; the Tangi Times carries Tangipahoa notices.

Why that matters to you: once you have taken fifteen minutes on a police jury agenda, your name is in the minutes — and the official journal prints the minutes. You have earned coverage in the parish paper without ever pitching a reporter. Do the agenda ask first; the press follows it for free.

The story bank — six things you can actually pitch

“We exist” is not a story. These are.

Story 1

The unit comes to town

A mobile outreach unit arriving in a parish that has never had one. Visual, local, dated. The easiest first placement and the one every paper takes.

Story 2

The coalition is named

Six local organisations sign an agreement to work together for veterans. Names the mayor can be quoted in, and the exact story a rural funder later wants to see published.

Story 3

One veteran, one outcome

The strongest story you will ever have — and the one you cannot tell until it is true and consented to. Never fabricate, never composite. Wait for it.

Story 4

Guardian Light

A congregation submits its veterans to the Wall of Honor. Families do the outreach for you and the story belongs to them, which is why it travels.

Story 5

The clerk of court partnership

A small operational story with an unusually good hook: the parish office where veterans already prove who they are is now handing them a way to get what they earned.

Story 6

Ready before the storm

Written into the parish emergency plan. Runs itself every hurricane season and positions the enterprise as infrastructure rather than charity.

What to say — and what not to say yet

This one is not optional

The Impact page shows 312 veterans reached, 38 M-TOC deployments, 23 active partners and similar figures, labelled plainly as pilot demo figures from the VI-PAR dashboard simulation — not yet independently verified. That labelling is honest and it should stay.

Those numbers must never appear on a flyer, in a mayor’s office, in a press release, or in a grant application. Demo data that escapes its disclaimer becomes a misrepresentation the moment somebody repeats it as fact. In a parish where your entire asset is that people believe you, that is the one unrecoverable mistake — and it is unrecoverable because the correction never travels as far as the claim.

Say this instead. All of it is true.

Four legally distinct entities operating together · a deployable mobile outreach unit · eight coordinated programmes · a documented navigation doctrine · a published twenty-three-document governance library · named federal and local partners on the resource map · and a reporting system built to publish verified outcomes as soon as they exist.

That is a stronger pitch than a borrowed number, because every line of it survives a follow-up question.

The one move this month

Publish ninety days of M-TOC dates. Not a strategy, not a brochure — a calendar. Three months of “where and when” on the website, on a printable sheet, and in the hand of every pastor, post commander and clerk you meet.

Every other line on this page is an amplifier. Without the dates there is nothing to amplify.
